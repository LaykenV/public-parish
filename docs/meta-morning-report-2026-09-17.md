## What the research added

The original evidence bundle contained seven official sources, including the
short description of the original utility order. This session retrieved the
full 44-page public order and its public settlement attachment through the
shared Firecrawl intake. The new bundle retains all seven earlier sources.

The full order supports specific explanations of protections for other
electricity customers:

- Material transmission upgrades needed solely for the customer project cannot
  enter other customers' rates.
- Entergy must obtain and enforce parent guarantees and other financial security.
- If the customer terminates its agreement, Entergy must seek value for excess
  capacity. After receiving an early termination fee, it must seek commission
  approval for the proposed use of that money.
- Entergy must report construction schedules and costs quarterly. Actual
  generator costs remain subject to later prudence review.
- Commission staff explicitly did not recommend approval of the electric
  service agreement and related contracts. The commission retained oversight
  of Entergy's management of those agreements.

These requirements apply to the original project. They do not prove that a
household's total bill cannot rise, or that the later expansion has approval.
See [Order U-37425, public PDF](https://lpscpubvalence.lpsc.louisiana.gov/portal/PSC/ViewFile?fileId=nDWn%2Fjuc2%2BA%3D),
especially printed pages 6 and 7.

The retained [July 13 LED announcement](https://www.opportunitylouisiana.gov/news/meta-commits-more-than-50-billion-for-north-louisiana-project-becoming-one-of-the-largest-data-centers-in-history)
also supports more useful local context. It reports contracts with Louisiana
businesses, school revenue, bill-assistance and efficiency commitments, and a
water-restoration commitment. Those remain attributed announcements. They do
not establish payments received, a particular teacher's bonus, measured water
use, or actual household savings.
